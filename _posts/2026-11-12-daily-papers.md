1. **Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks** - 👍 26
   - 기관: Intern Large Models1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40394)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40394)
   - Abstract: Mathematical supervision for language models is typically flattened into isolated problem-solution pairs, discarding the curricular organization and evidential structure of the original sources. We introduce TOME-Math (Textbook-Oriented Mathematical Evidence), a large-scale corpus that converts 2,118 textbooks spanning the core areas of university-level mathematics subjects into approximately 380K structured proof-supervision records while preserving chapter order, proposition-proof links, dependency references, and source provenance. Leveraging these structural signals, we explore curriculum learning across multiple mathematical subjects and characterize scaling patterns with supervision volume and source breadth. At full scale, fine-tuning on TOME-Math raises the unweighted mean score across our held-out evaluations and established public benchmarks by 16.7 percentage points over the base model, while exceeding all three public instruction-data baselines evaluated in aggregate. Our findings highlight structured textbooks as both a scalable source of mathematical supervision and an empirical framework for studying the impact of data organization on mathematical reasoning. We will release the corpus to facilitate further research on structured mathematical supervision.

2. **Incentivizing Self-Learning in Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning** - 👍 15
   - 기관: Intern Large Models3
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39671)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39671)
   - Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) draw on parametric knowledge and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to solve knowledge-intensive tasks. Yet problem solving in unfamiliar domains calls for tightly coupled knowledge acquisition and reasoning, beyond factual retrieval and aggregation or learning solely from a preassembled context. We study self-learning -- autonomously retrieving, studying, and applying knowledge from long-form reference materials on the fly. To investigate this capability, we formulate a textbook-based mathematical problem-solving task. LLMs must navigate textbook exposition, learn and apply relevant principles, and construct proofs for exercises, with reference solutions withheld. We introduce a BookNavigator interface to support this human-inspired study process and incentivize self-learning through reinforcement learning (RL) with fine-grained rewards. Experiments show that our method improves performance across in-domain and out-of-domain textbooks and subjects, raising Intern-S2-Preview-35B's overall proof accuracy from 51.5% to 79.5% and outperforming RL-trained textbook-free and fixed-retrieval baselines. These gains extend to complex long-context benchmarks involving different task types, with improvements over the base model of 1.7 and 2.3 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus and CL-bench, respectively. Together, these findings suggest that RL can foster a domain-agnostic self-learning strategy, highlighting the potential of self-learning from long-form corpora to strengthen broader problem-solving capabilities on complex, long-context tasks.

3. **SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering** - 👍 13
   - 기관: ·6 authors1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13410)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13410)
   - Abstract: Low-bit quantization reduces the inference cost of LLM agents but can compromise task success. Examining failed rollouts, we find that quantized agents overlook task requirements or environmental conditions, producing environment transitions that diverge from successful full-precision references. Correcting these behavioral divergences remains difficult: offline data may miss the student's failure contexts, while its own rollouts may provide few correct responses in those contexts. We introduce SteerDistill, a quantization-aware distillation method in which a full-precision teacher guides the student to revise failed responses and iteratively refines its guidance using feedback from student execution. This closed-loop teacher steering produces locally verified behavioral corrections. We internalize these corrections by training the student without guidance to match the steered student's predictions at corrected turns and the full-precision teacher's at other turns, enabling unassisted inference. Across five agentic benchmarks and three models, SteerDistill exceeds the strongest quantized baseline by 1.67--2.18 points in average score under W4A16 and stays within one point of BF16. On $\tau^2$-Bench, it outperforms QeRL by 1.84 percentage points in success rate using 67% fewer training GPU hours and produces shorter successful trajectories than the quantized baselines.

4. **GTAGym: Real AAA Game Tasks Environment for Evaluation and Reinforcement Learning** - 👍 10
   - 기관: OpenMOSS13
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13028)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13028)
   - Abstract: AAA games offer a rich testbed for evaluating agent perception, reasoning, and control. However, evaluating whether agents can complete tasks in AAA games is challenging and often human-annotated because task outcomes are often difficult to verify automatically, let alone applying it for Reinforcement Learning (RL). We introduce GTAGym, an environment and framework for evaluation and RL on GTA V tasks. Its evaluation benchmark, GTAVBench, derived from GTA V main-story missions, contains 103 online tasks reconstructed across nine task families, alongside 47 image-based shooting and sniping tasks using GTA V imagery. A web server provides access to the online environment and evaluation interface, supporting parallel testing through screenshots and keyboard-mouse actions with automatic engine-state outcome checks. We evaluate six frontier LLMs on GTAVBench with textual task instructions and on its GTAVBench-AGI branch, where instructions appear only in the first screenshot and a harness automatically compresses interaction history. The best configuration achieves 70.9% overall accuracy in GTAVBench-AGI, yet aerial navigation, evasion and interception remain difficult. GTAGym also provides a scalable generator that samples task parameters within user-specified ranges and option sets under fixed templates, creating batches of 3D and image-based shooting environments for RL with automatic reward and termination signals. LoRA RL of Qwen3.8-27B on generated on-foot navigation tasks improves the in-domain performance and transfers to out-of-domain task categories, and native GTA V tasks from 15% to 23%.

5. **Ensemble Routing for Prompt-Adaptive Cost-Aware LLM Inference** - 👍 7
   - 기관: ·4 authors1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10193)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10193)
   - Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly support Web-based services that must balance response quality and inference cost across heterogeneous requests. Conventional routing selects one model per prompt, while fixed ensembles cannot adapt their composition and cost to individual requests. We introduce PISA, a prompt-adaptive, cost-aware single-model router, and PISA-E, its extension to ensemble routing. At the core of both methods, PISA combines soft cluster memberships with model-specific predictors to estimate individual model capabilities. Building on these estimates, PISA-E jointly selects ensemble composition and cardinality under an explicit cost--quality preference that can be adjusted at inference time without retraining. A tractable ensemble-success surrogate and efficient candidate selection enable this extension without ensemble-specific training. Across four LLM routing benchmarks under in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions, PISA achieves the highest macro-average cost--performance hypervolume among the evaluated single-model routers in both settings. Meanwhile, PISA-E achieves the highest macro-average task performance among the evaluated ensemble approaches when prioritizing response quality, surpassing the strongest competitors by 2.09 and 2.87 percentage points under ID and OOD conditions, respectively. At selected lower-cost operating points, it also exceeds the strongest ensemble competitors' macro-average task performance while reducing average inference cost by approximately 36% and 25%, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of prompt-adaptive capability estimation for both single-model routing and selective ensembling.

6. **Read Less, Solve More: Token-Efficient Sparse Reading of External Content for AI Agents** - 👍 4
   - 기관: Institute of Computing Technology1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22237)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22237)
   - Abstract: Web-facing AI agents repeatedly access webpages, documents, repositories, and tool responses, yet reading interfaces often expose entire objects when only sparse evidence is needed. This over-reading increases token and latency costs and can dilute relevant evidence. We present SparseRead, a reusable reading control layer that governs content admission without modifying the agent model. Its Read Intent Protocol tracks evolving needs and source coverage; Adaptive Reader Backends select how evidence is located and exposed; and the Budgeted Read Gate bounds cumulative raw exposure per episode with faithful full-view fallback. A unified Reader interface supports interchangeable evidence acquisition across heterogeneous resources. Across six models, including Claude Opus 5, and five scenarios, SparseRead reduces total tokens by up to 92.9% and wall time by up to 89.0%, preserving or improving task quality in 97% settings. On Web deep-search tasks, it reduces tokens by 31.0--43.0% and task completion time by 23.0--37.0%. Ablations validate the three designs, while integration with three agent frameworks demonstrates portability.

7. **CogBattery: Human-Referenced Evaluation of Cognitive Task Behavior and Structure in Language Models** - 👍 2
   - 기관: ·4 authors01
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24999)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24999)
   - Abstract: Human cognitive tasks let us compare language models with humans under controlled conditions. Yet a model can reproduce the direction of a human effect while making different errors. CogBattery evaluates 54 open-weight language models on text adaptations of 13 cognitive paradigms, comparing each with human data from its source study. Like humans, models respond more to n-back lures than to controls, yet they make far more false alarms on ordinary non-targets. Most models' reported confidence separates correct from incorrect answers less well than humans' does, and most always choose the same wager, including models that classify accurately. These patterns span many model families. Across tasks, model scores share two dimensions. The first is closely tied to scale, yet larger models are not consistently closer to humans. Predefined cognitive groupings show no reliable predictive advantage over random groupings of the same size. These results show that matching human effect directions does not establish human-like behavior. CogBattery provides condition-level human references for testing errors and confidence reports in new models.

8. **WMIP: A Working-Memory-Inspired Programmable Probe of Cumulative State Tracking in Large Language Models** - 👍 2
   - 기관: ·6 authors11
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.27343)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27343)
   - Abstract: Human working memory lets people maintain and update information. Large language models (LLMs) face a related demand when tracking updates within a single prompt without writing out intermediate states, yet existing tests differ in their constraints on written steps. We introduce a working-memory-inspired probe of cumulative state tracking with program-computed answers, 3 to 100 updates, and final-state, intermediate-state, or multi-target queries. It compares protocols on identical items. Across 18 LLMs, accuracy is 84.9% on single-step arithmetic but 31.7% on core items requiring one number after three to seven updates. From 50 updates on, no model exceeds 10% accuracy. Core accuracy reaches 88.0% under a larger budget and instructions to write steps and place the answer on the last line, mainly due to the instruction changes rather than the budget. Core accuracy also correlates positively with public Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard scores. These findings show that strong single-step arithmetic does not ensure reliable cumulative tracking and that output protocols substantially change measured accuracy. We release the generator, items, scoring code, and responses for reproducible comparisons across depths, query types, and protocols.

9. **Mixed Signals: Weight-Space Programming for EEG and ECG** - 👍 1
   - 기관: EPFL1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13758)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13758)
   - Abstract: Weight-space programming allows language models to read a trained neural network's weights and edit them. This opens the door to ``training'' by reasoning rather than through gradient descent. Prior work has demonstrated its functionality and promise for game-playing networks and fixing specific failures in language models, even extending to diffusion models. In this work, we present its applicability to models trained on biosignal data, specifically EEG and ECG data. We show that frontier models are not only able to debug specific faults planted in biosignal-based models without being told what these faults are, but are even capable of writing all the weights of a network from scratch, given only the architecture, the task, and a minimal set of training subjects. In particular, in one set of experiments, a language model was able to match standard gradient-descent training using $6\times$ fewer training subjects, through reasoning alone and without any gradient descent step. Networks written by Claude Opus 5.5 also scored higher than classic hand-crafted EEG pipelines (alpha band power, and common spatial patterns with linear discriminant analysis) when built from the same 5 subjects. We also highlight some of the limitations, which include the inability of language models to improve already well-trained classification models, and their incapacity to reliably recognize, from the weights alone, the task a network was trained on. Overall, our results indicate that weight-space programming can be useful for small networks, such as those deployed on edge devices and wearables, but the concept still requires further investigation before it can be used in practice.

10. **Module-Aware Optimization for Graph Neural Networks** - 👍 1
   - 기관: ·4 authors1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08379)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08379)
   - Abstract: Different optimizers impose different geometries on parameter updates. Adaptive methods such as Adam and AdamW use coordinate-wise updates, while matrix-aware optimizers such as Muon utilize weight-matrix structure through orthogonalized updates. For Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), however, the effective geometry of its weight matrices is shaped not only by its learned channel-mixing weights, but also by the graph-propagated representations it acts on. Thus, even if an update is well normalized as a matrix, its effect on the layer output can be amplified when applied to graph-propagated features. To address this gap, we introduce GraphMuon, a graph-aware optimizer that adapts Muon's matrix updates to the covariance structure of graph-propagated layer inputs. GraphMuon performs orthogonalization in a metric defined by these propagated representations and normalizes step sizes according to the update's induced effect on the layer representation. We show that theoretically, vanilla Muon can suffer from graph-induced update amplification, while our GraphMuon can mitigate this effect. As such, our GraphMuon yields a graph-metric analogue of Muon's polar update step. Empirically, GraphMuon achieves $1.32\times$-$2.42\times$ validation-loss time-to-target speedups over AdamW on three large-scale OGB benchmarks. Across 15 dataset-architecture configurations spanning node classification, inductive graph classification, and graph regression, it achieves lower time-to-target in 13 cases, while maintaining competitive or superior predictive performance.
