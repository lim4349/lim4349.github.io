1. **Real2SimLab: A Unified Platform for Real-to-Simulation Transfer** - 👍 101
   - 기관: PKU-VaLuE-Lab212
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34849)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34849)
   - Abstract: Real-to-simulation transfer, which builds simulations from real-world observations, has long been studied in research and industry. Recently, coding agents have become capable of generating simulation programs from video recordings, and several benchmarks have been proposed to evaluate them. However, each individual benchmark defines its own inputs, deliverables, and scoring pipeline. This leads to $N \times M$ integrations when evaluating $N$ methods on $M$ benchmarks. To this end, we present Real2SimLab, a unified platform for real-to-simulation transfer. It evaluates a method by running its written program and scoring the output simulation with the original scorer of each benchmark. Moreover, Real2SimLab provides a pipeline to create training data and reward annotations for real-to-simulation methods. Our pipeline carefully avoids data contamination by filtering out the video recordings that exist in benchmarks. For each video recording, a program is first produced, and then our pipeline computes its reward with the same code that Real2SimLab uses for evaluation. So far, it supports 47 coding agents and 5 non-agent methods on 6 benchmarks. Both the platform and Awesome-Real2Sim, our research map of nearly 300 real-to-simulation papers, are open source, and we invite the Real2SimHub community to build them together.

2. **Sage-Med: Scaling Medical Multimodal Intelligence** - 👍 84
   - 기관: Shanghai AI Laboratory191
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34575)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34575)
   - Abstract: Establishing effective scaling laws for medical multimodal large language models (MLLMs) remains a persistent and fundamental challenge. Our systematic evaluation shows that existing medical MLLMs tend to underperform frontier general-purpose MLLMs across a range of medical tasks, while scaling model size primarily improves text capabilities rather than multimodal perception, and enabling reasoning modes fails to close this visual gap. To investigate how medical multimodal intelligence scales in practice, we introduce Sage-Med-Preview, a 35B-parameter medical multimodal model with 3B activated parameters, built by combining stage-specific curation over 322.7 million candidate records with a progressive training curriculum spanning medical pretraining, staged supervised fine-tuning, and adaptive reinforcement learning. Across this curriculum, progressive supervised refinement and adaptive reinforcement learning yield consistent stage-wise gains, substantially advancing both specialized visual understanding and medical language capabilities. Evaluated against 77 competing models across 238 clinical tasks spanning 61 public and 13 private multi-center benchmarks in more than 40 modalities, Sage-Med-Preview achieves an average score of 58.26% on the 31 public multimodal benchmarks. It outperforms the state-of-the-art open-source medical model Hulu-Med-235B-A22B at 50.99% and the general scientific model Intern-S2-Preview at 52.24%, and approaches the advanced proprietary model GPT-6.2-Sol across multiple tasks such as comprehensive multimodal understanding, report generation, and visual grounding. Although substantial challenges remain in complex medical reasoning and multi-step clinical workflows, this report provides empirical evidence, actionable data and training recipes, and a rigorous benchmark toward scalable clinical multimodal intelligence.

3. **Mixture-of-Loops Transformer** - 👍 57
   - 기관: ByteDance Seed691
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34923)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923)
   - Abstract: Looped Transformers boost expressivity by reusing shared layers across loops, yet at the cost of efficiency. Pushing their expressivity-efficiency frontier requires rethinking the core mechanism connecting adjacent loops: What should be passed as a Transformer loops? We introduce Mixture-of-Loops Transformer (MoLT), whose Mixture-of-Loops Attention (MoLA) uses keys and values (KV) as memory for selective reuse across depths and loops. Under loop residual, MoLT-R retains the residual stream and reads local KV across loops for expressivity. Under loop KV, MoLT-KV instead restarts from embeddings and reads current-loop local KV with preceding-loop memory KV at earlier positions for efficiency. MoLT-KV eliminates same-position cross-loop dependence and, paired with loop forcing for parallel training, unlocks single-traversal Mixture-of-Loops (MoL) decoding beyond the training loop horizon. To balance efficiency and quality, we introduce U-shaped MoLT (U-MoLT), which separates memory construction from prediction readout and skips unused decoder traversals without changing predictions. In 400B-token pretraining, U-MoLT outperforms OLMo3 across 700M/1.5B dense and 3B MoE models, achieving 1.7x validation cross-entropy (CE) token efficiency at 700M while improving 3B MoE 10-domain perplexity by 0.75 and 10-task accuracy by 2.1 percentage points. In 100B-token continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base, our U-MoLT adaptation lowers CE on all 11 domains over the matched baseline with 1.4x mean token efficiency. At inference, U-MoLT with MoL-Hybrid-2 matches non-looped OLMo3 latency at 996.6 vs. 1,000.6 ms on S-size (~113M) models. Code is available at this https URL

4. **DyLoMo-DiT: Dynamic Looped Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion Transformers** - 👍 55
   - 기관: ·4 authors413
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30844)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30844)
   - Abstract: Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) scale model capacity by increasing both parameter count and computation. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) variants expand parameter capacity while keeping per-token computation roughly fixed, but still require substantial parameter storage. In this paper, we propose Dynamic Looped MoE DiT (DyLoMo-DiT), which reuses parameters rather than adding them: a weight-shared loop repeatedly applies the middle layers, while sparse experts allocate parameter capacity across tokens. This design deepens the network without adding parameters, freeing the parameter budget for other uses, such as more experts or more distinct layers. It also allocates compute dynamically, varying the number of passes across noise levels. Experiments show that DyLoMo-DiT makes more effective use of parameter and compute budgets, with further gains from assigning more passes to low-noise steps. On ImageNet $256\times256$, DyLoMo-DiT reduces FID by 11-24% relative to DiT and 36-56% relative to DiT-MoE at matched parameter and compute budgets. For text-to-image generation, a scaled DyLoMo-DiT achieves a higher GenEval score than FLUX.1 (0.714 vs. 0.673), with less than one tenth of its active parameters (1.05B vs. 12B).

5. **VibeVideoAgent: Closing the Perceptual Loop for Code-Driven Video Generation** - 👍 54
   - 기관: Kling-Keye54
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34237)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34237)
   - Abstract: Code-driven video generation asks a coding agent to write and run rendering programs. This gives exact control over typography, data visualizations, synchronized audio, and multi-shot scenes, and it leaves an editable project behind. A program that exits cleanly can still render overlapping labels, a camera aimed away from its subject, broken object interactions, or narration that drifts out of sync, and none of these raise a runtime error. Feeding rendered frames and audio back into the coding session bloats its long-horizon context, while critiquing only the finished full-resolution render turns early design mistakes into costly rewrites. We introduce VibeVideoAgent, a training-free framework that lets coding agents inspect their drafts throughout production. Multimodal perception runs in short-lived contexts kept apart from the coder and returns compact, grounded Findings. Seven primitives operate on a unified multi-clock representation of assets and drafts: they explore media from coarse to fine, verify quoted requirements with evidence from every required modality, compare a draft with a reference or an earlier revision on separate clocks, and measure stream signals locally. A lightweight staged protocol has the agent test its riskiest decision on a minimal preview first. On all 239 VibeVideoBench tasks, with matched agent runtimes and a fixed perception model, VibeVideoAgent improves holistic audiovisual quality (L3) for Claude Opus 5.5 (4.03 -> 4.43, while L2 rubric compliance rises from 98.87% to 99.35%), GPT-6 Astra (4.03 -> 4.27), and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (3.97 -> 4.10), with the largest gains in audio-visual synchronization (up to +0.56). Controlled ablations show that each component contributes, and trajectory audits show that the granularity of code edits governs the trade-off between rubric adherence and perceptual quality.

6. **Improving and Expanding Video Editing Capabilities Without Videos** - 👍 43
   - 기관: Carnegie Mellon University School of Computer Science292
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34941)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34941)
   - Abstract: Image editing models can follow a wide range of instructions, but video editing models still lag behind, limited in part by the difficulty of collecting paired source and edited videos. Is it possible to transfer advanced image editing capabilities to video editors without new paired videos? We introduce Image-to-Video Transfer (I2VT), which improves pretrained video editors and expands them to new editing tasks using an image editor. It enables efficient post-training with no architectural changes and no new videos, using only individual images. Our key idea is to let the video editor role-play as an image editor: it edits an image as a one-frame video, with the image editor guiding the video editor's predictions at each denoising step. Although the video editor sees only single images during training, at test time it applies the improved and expanded capabilities to whole video clips while preserving temporal consistency. To push the boundaries of video editing, we introduce OpenVE++, an extension of OpenVE-Bench that adds four new editing tasks useful for video production, including action changes, expression changes, body movements, and scene relighting. Our experiments across three different video editors show that I2VT improves existing editing capabilities and expands them to new tasks, and our models achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on both OpenVE-Bench and OpenVE++.

7. **PyRUA-Dex: Learning Programmatic Policies for Dexterous Bimanual Manipulation with Coding Agents** - 👍 43
   - 기관: DAGroup-PKU2
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33144)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33144)
   - Abstract: Learning for dexterous manipulation is limited by the scarcity of high-quality demonstrations: retargeting from human video is imprecise, teleoperation gives no force feedback to the operator, and exoskeleton-based collection is hard to scale up. We present PyRUA-Dex, a framework that enables coding agents to develop dexterous manipulation through programmatic policy, without the need of demonstrations. The programmatic policy is developed in two stages: during exploration, multiple agents explore in parallel for task skills and ways to compose them, and a distilling agent condenses the successful attempts into a task library and one program. During refinement, this program is improved through randomized layouts for enhanced robustness and execution efficiency. In simulated environments, PyRUA-Dex achieves SOTA on Bench2Dex with a score of 72.9 and reaches 81.0% success on DexJoCo under full randomization, ahead of every trained policy on both benchmarks. In real world experiments on a bimanual platform with two 22-DoF hands, PyRUA-Dex learns to play piano, write calligraphy, and other tasks efficiently: learning a new piece from sheet music takes as little as 30 minutes. These results suggest that PyRUA-Dex is an effective framework for developing dexterous manipulation policies with coding agents in the absence of high quality dexterous manipulation demonstrations.

8. **DocSEAM: Bidirectional Cross-Page Encoding for Multi-Page Document Understanding** - 👍 26
   - 기관: MMLab@NTU193
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34420)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34420)
   - Abstract: Multi-page document vision-language models encode each page independently, creating a feature-level discontinuity at page boundaries: visual tokens are formed without access to content on adjacent pages, and the language model must reason over these page representations formed in isolation. We introduce DocSEAM (Document Selective Enrichment via Anchored Masking), a lightweight module inserted between a pretrained vision encoder and the language model that injects the inductive bias of local cross-page context into visual representations. For each page, DocSEAM forms a local triplet with its two neighbors, applies center-anchored masked self-attention with directional salience gates to fuse information from neighbor pages, and returns only the enriched center-page tokens, leaving the visual token count unchanged. After a supervised cross-page warm-up and multi-page fine-tuning with DocSEAM frozen, our 9B-parameter model achieves state-of-the-art results among the evaluated open-weight models of up to 10B parameters on five multi-page document VQA benchmarks. We further show that under controlled re-pagination, DocSEAM reduces sensitivity to boundary placement and improves cross-page span localization

9. **HOG-WAM: Improving Visual Robustness through Future Gradient Prediction** - 👍 22
   - 기관: intelligence++51
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34811)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34811)
   - Abstract: World-action models jointly predict future observations and actions, yet visual corruption can disrupt the resulting policies. We introduce HOG-WAM, which adds future Histograms of Oriented Gradients (HOG) as structural supervision derived directly from demonstration images. An auxiliary HOG diffusion transformer (DiT) module reads RGB features through asymmetric attention, allowing its objective to shape the visual representation used for control. The module is used only during training and removed at inference, preserving FastWAM's RGB-to-action architecture. We evaluate four controlled observation corruptions through a visual-corruption extension of RoboTwin, termed RoboTwin-Plus; HOG-WAM achieves the highest success among the compared policies under all four. Mixed-noise success increases from 78.86% to 82.86% relative to FastWAM, with comparable performance on clean scenes. On LIBERO Plus, overall success increases from 74.49% to 75.26%, with the largest gain under sensor noise, from 74.14% to 78.89%. Across four Franka tasks, average success increases from 40% to 59% under clean observations and from 17% to 24% under noisy observations. A representation ablation favors combining RGB observations with auxiliary HOG prediction over replacing RGB with HOG. These findings support future-gradient prediction as complementary supervision for visually robust control, with larger absolute gains on physical tasks than in simulation. 10. **EgoUseBench: Benchmarking Computer-Use Agents under Evolving First-Person Contexts** - 👍 19
   - 기관: Zhejiang University261
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34466)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34466)
   - Abstract: AI wearables, such as smart glasses, extend the context available to agents beyond digital environments to users' physical surroundings. With these devices, computer tasks can originate from users' ongoing activities rather than solely from explicit instructions. Through first-person observations, agents can identify objects and activities relevant to user requests and ground digital tasks in the surrounding context. We call this interaction paradigm Ego Use. However, first-person observations are limited by the wearer's viewpoint and may provide incomplete or ambiguous evidence of ongoing activities. As new observations arrive, agents may need to revise their understanding of the physical context and user intent, and reassess whether prior digital actions remain valid. Whether current agents can reliably handle these changes remains insufficiently evaluated. We introduce EgoUseBench, a benchmark for evaluating Ego Use across three interaction settings with different levels of first-person context. Computer-Use relies on explicit instructions, Offline Ego Use provides prerecorded first-person context before execution, and Online Ego Use provides continuous observations as user intent evolves. Based on over 74 hours of real-world first-person recordings, we construct 693 tasks across the three interaction settings. To capture task evolution, we track first-person observations, user requirements, and website state separately. We further group tasks by how information changes during execution, resulting in three types: Graft (revised user requirements), Refresh (new first-person observations), and Clarify (agent-initiated information seeking). We evaluate advanced models and find that average success rates in Online Ego Use are 26.3% lower than in Computer-Use. This result reveals the difficulty of keeping task execution aligned with evolving first-person context and user intent.
