1. **Tideway: Phase-Aware Tiered Memory Management for MoE Inference on Consumer Devices** - 👍 60
   - 기관: Duke University41
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14848)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14848)
   - Abstract: Large language model (LLM) agents are rapidly becoming a dominant paradigm for AI applications. Recently, there is a growing demand to deploy large-scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models locally on consumer PCs to balance data privacy, API costs, and inference efficiency. However, unlike datacenter servers with abundant DRAM, consumer devices are tightly constrained in both GPU VRAM and host RAM, forcing massive MoE weights to spill across VRAM, host RAM, and SSD. Under such spilling, we observe that existing systems fail to fully utilize this memory hierarchy, treating the tiers as independent caches and thrashing in inference. We present TIDEWAY, a system for efficient inference of large MoE models on memory-constrained consumer devices. Our core insight is to manage host RAM and GPU VRAM as a unified memory pool. Within this pool, TIDEWAY couples phase-aware memory layouts, which adapt to the distinct access patterns of prefill and decode, with an execution engine that optimizes CUDA Graph execution under expert paging. Our evaluations on representative consumer hardware with frontier MoE models demonstrate that TIDEWAY significantly outperforms state-of-the-art systems. On average, TIDEWAY achieves speedups of 1.97x in end-to-end latency and 2.40x in Time-to-First-Token (TTFT), along with 1.61x higher decode throughput against the fastest baseline. 2. **Jeveration: System One Models Now Generate** - 👍 35
   - 기관: National University of Singapore152
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24738)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24738)
   - Abstract: System One models such as Jev return decisions through fast, parallel prediction. In this paper, we introduce Jeveration, showing that the same decision paradigm can also generate high-quality images in a single forward pass. Jeveration builds on a frozen vision-language model, repurposing its visual-semantic knowledge for holistic image generation. The key challenge is to maintain global consistency. A single prompt admits many valid images, yet parallel predictions must converge on a coherent whole. We therefore condition all positions on a shared random state to coordinate parallel predictions, so that each draw yields a different but coherent image. Since the model tends to ignore this state under direct image supervision, we first learn a distribution over discrete image codes to guide single-pass generation. Beyond text-to-image generation, we further unify image-to-image generation and dense prediction within the same architecture, so that one checkpoint supports all three tasks, each in one forward pass. Experiments show that Jeveration generates a 512x512 image in 78 ms, up to 122x faster than existing unified models, while maintaining competitive image quality and dense prediction performance.

3. **PROLA: Principal-Orthogonal Low-rank Adaptation for Predictive Spatiotemporal Weather Downscaling** - 👍 26
   - 기관: ·4 authors11
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11318)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11318)
   - Abstract: High-resolution weather prediction is important for resolving local atmospheric patterns missed by coarse global forecasts. Large pretrained weather backbones have improved global forecasting, but adapting them to produce future high-resolution fields from recent coarse states remains challenging and costly. We formulate this setting as Predictive Spatiotemporal Weather Downscaling (PSWD), a task that jointly refines spatial and temporal resolution while predicting future high-resolution trajectories. To this end, we propose a framework that decomposes prediction into spatial and temporal stages using a shared pretrained backbone, fixed numerical scaffolds, and lightweight residual heads. For backbone adaptation, we introduce PROLA (PRincipal-Orthogonal Low-rank Adaptation), which allocates a fixed low-rank budget between pretrained weights' principal directions and their orthogonal complement. PROLA further rescales rank-1 optimizer updates using gradient signals, improving adaptation without increasing trainable rank. Experiments in the PSWD setting show that PROLA outperforms representative low-rank adaptation baselines. Further experiments across diverse datasets and tasks support the broader applicability of PROLA, showing that its benefits extend beyond the primary weather downscaling setting.

4. **Game2Player: Scaling Gameplay Experience for Visual Agents through Environment-Player Co-Development** - 👍 22
   - 기관: University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign211
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24154)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24154)
   - Abstract: Games are a rich testbed for visual agents, demanding perception, long-horizon planning, and precise control. However, collecting human gameplay is costly, and learning from gameplay videos requires inferring missing actions. Existing datasets also cover a limited range of games, often without explicit tasks or verifiable outcomes. To address these gaps, we introduce Game2Player, a framework that co-develops game environments and players to collect diverse and verifiable gameplay experience at scale. In the Game2Player loop, a Builder agent adapts existing web games, exposes their internal state, and defines tasks with state-based progress measures and success checks. A Coding Player and a VLM Player then attempt the tasks and report problems to the Builder for repair, so that play serves as both environment validation and training data collection. With Game2Player, we build 568 verified web games with 3,436 tasks, collect 15.7K trajectories (307 hours of gameplay), and hold out 50 games as the benchmark PlayerBench. To evaluate the collected experience as training data, we train VLM agents with progress-weighted SFT, assigning greater weight to steps that advance task progress. Game2Player-9B improves progress from 14.3% to 31.2% on PlayerBench and from 30.0% to 53.1% on the external GameWorld benchmark. More broadly, our work suggests a path for visual agents to scale their gameplay experience by building, verifying, and learning from existing game worlds.

5. **ALIVE: Making Streaming Video Models Interactive** - 👍 19
   - 기관: Tsinghua University101
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21366)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21366)
   - Abstract: Streaming video LLMs can continuously observe the world, yet often handle user requests in isolation. We introduce ALIVE, an interactive streaming video model supporting proactive monitoring, streaming task coordination, and feedback-seeking interaction. We construct ALIVE-SFT-288K and ALIVE-RL-3K for two-stage training. Contrastive supervised fine-tuning teaches when and how to respond, while reinforcement learning with Behavior-Aligned Credit Assignment (BACA) combines trajectory-level and local advantages to refine decisions, responses, and silence across multi-turn interactions. We also introduce FSI-Bench to evaluate feedback seeking and subsequent task completion. ALIVE achieves the highest overall scores among the compared models on four public benchmarks and FSI-Bench, demonstrating strong streaming understanding and interactive capabilities.

6. **The Resolution Hypothesis: Agent Evolution's Key Is Identifying Improvements** - 👍 17
   - 기관: ShanghaiTech University1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23068)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23068)
   - Abstract: Agent evolution follows a simple loop: a proposer writes candidate artifacts (prompts, skills, memories) and selection accepts those that score higher. This loop has shown promise, yet practitioners face a puzzle: methods trade wins across papers, and evolution often fails to bootstrap on certain tasks. We trace this puzzle to the Resolution Hypothesis: agent evolution stalls not because methods fail to propose better artifacts, but because noisy evaluations cannot reliably identify them. Across seven evolution methods and six models (over 10M LLM calls), we find support for the hypothesis at both levels where evaluation scores are used. At the candidate level (choosing artifacts), helpful candidate artifacts exist, but selection is no better than random at telling them from harmful ones, so errors accumulate across rounds. At the method level (ranking methods), typical margins of a few percentage points are smaller than the difference between two independent runs of the same method, so published rankings often rest on luck. Together, these findings indicate that agent evolution is often bottlenecked by reliably identifying improvements. This calls for a shift in how we develop and evaluate self-improving agents, from designing ever more candidate improvement systems to making true improvements detectable and accumulable.

7. **WorldRuler: Interactive World Modeling under Configurable Rules** - 👍 15
   - 기관: Alaya Lab61
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24869)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24869)
   - Abstract: Rules govern how player actions change interactive worlds. Different rules create distinct gameplay experiences within similar scenes and action spaces. For customizable gameplay, generative world models must follow user-defined rules beyond real-world behaviors. However, realizing user-defined rules presents two challenges: Rule-State Persistence and Rule-to-Visual Realization. The former requires preserving rule definitions, dependencies, and world states across saving and loading, so that restored worlds follow the established rules. The latter requires accurately realizing rule-induced changes on designated objects and attributes, particularly when affected objects are unseen. In this paper, we propose WorldRuler, which organizes rule definitions, dependencies, and object states in an executable 3D rule graph and connects rule execution, video generation, and perceptual feedback in a closed loop. The graph supports joint saving and restoration of rules and world states, while dynamic object binding grounds rule-specified entities in visual instances and uses stored states to constrain generation from their first appearance. We further introduce object attribute state replacement and counterfactual reconstruction training, enabling accurate attribute control and compatible appearance and motion changes while limiting interference with unrelated content. We develop an automated data collection and annotation pipeline using GTA-V and Habitat to construct RuleVerse, a dataset of rule-driven interactions with detailed state and event annotations, including cases where rules take effect before affected objects become visible. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of WorldRuler in preserving rule-state consistency across saving and loading and accurately realizing rule outcomes on designated objects and attributes.

8. **CurvDelta: Low-Curvature Delta Modeling For Surgical Learning and Unlearning** - 👍 13
   - 기관: ·6 authors1
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24654)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24654)
   - Abstract: A central challenge in adapting large language models (LLMs) is capability preservation: an adaptation can achieve its target while degrading general capabilities. We introduce CurvDelta, a scalable framework that incorporates the preservation constraint into the parameterization of the model update. By analyzing the local geometry of the preservation constraint around the pre-adaptation model, CurvDelta constructs a low-curvature space in which parameter updates leave the preservation loss nearly invariant. We make its construction practical for LLMs through Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (K-FAC) and a factorized projection. We also introduce CurvLoRA, a low-rank variant within the same preservation geometry, designed for more efficient adaptation. Both methods apply to preservation-constrained adaptation tasks such as capability learning and selective unlearning. We evaluate our methods on mathematical reasoning, code generation, and knowledge unlearning. For mathematical reasoning and code generation, our approaches achieve competitive downstream performance while better preserving general capabilities, offering a favorable acquisition--retention trade-off relative to the baselines. For knowledge unlearning, our main results on TOFU and MUSE show a better balance between targeted forgetting and knowledge retention than the baselines, with retention performance close to the from-scratch retraining reference.

9. **Code World Model 2.0: Towards Infinite Controllable Video Generation** - 👍 10
   - 기관: inclusionAI1091
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22925)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22925)
   - Abstract: Visual proxies decouple explicit world-state simulation from photorealistic video synthesis, offering a controllable interface for both offline clip creation and real-time interactive streaming. Yet a practical renderer must address two challenges. Upstream proxies vary from coarse layouts to fine articulated geometry depending on the interaction, and streaming inference must operate in a few causal steps without accumulating drift across self-generated history. We present Invoker-Render, pairing a bidirectional creation model with a causal four-step streaming model under a shared conditioning interface over structural proxies, an appearance reference, and language. Trained on an aligned approximately 200-hour multi-granularity corpus, Invoker-Render honors fine geometric constraints when supplied while synthesizing plausible local motion under coarse proxies, enabling runtime selection of input detail. To bridge bidirectional creation and real-time streaming without compounding step compression and rollout drift, we introduce Progressive Forcing (ProForcing). ProForcing first trains a causal predictor and distills it into a four-step renderer using histories derived from training videos. Training then shifts to continuous self-generated rollouts. A pipelined serving stack supports efficient streaming inference. Experiments show versatile control across proxy families, granularities, and camera paths, consistent gains over recent streaming baselines, and stable 30-minute racing rollouts that preserve scene and subject appearance.

10. **HINT-WAM: Hindsight-Informed Training for World Action Models** - 👍 8
   - 기관: ·3 authors11
   - [HF 페이지](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24789)
   - [논문 링크](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24789)
   - Abstract: World action models (WAMs) have shown promising generalization and data efficiency in robot manipulation by coupling action generation with predictions of future visual dynamics. Translating these capabilities into reliable task execution, however, remains a challenge for existing post-training methods. Offline supervised fine-tuning provides dense supervision on expert trajectories, but its flow-matching action expert is trained only on noised demonstration actions and never on the intermediate states produced by its own sampler; task-specific adaptation can also compromise pretrained capabilities. On-policy reinforcement learning trains on the policy's own behavior, yet sparse task rewards make exploration and credit assignment difficult, while dense reward shaping still provides scalar evaluations rather than direct action targets. We introduce \textbf{HINT-WAM}, the first on-policy self-distillation framework that supervises the action expert on its own sampling trajectories using hindsight, the future video of the demonstration, as privileged information available to the WAM itself. For each demonstration observation, the student generates its action through its own denoising trajectory, and at the states along this trajectory the same model acts as a teacher conditioned additionally on this hindsight, providing a conditional action distribution as a dense training target, while the student sees only deployment-time inputs and needs no environmental rewards or simulator interaction. To transfer this supervision to a flow-matching action expert, HINT-WAM distills the teacher into the student with an energy score, a distribution-level objective that matches the teacher's action distribution from the student's own samples rather than regressing its velocity field.
